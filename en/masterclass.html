Create HYPERREALISTIC Consistent AI Characters

ComfyUI Mickmumpitz LoRA AI Toolkit

This page breaks down Mickmumpitz's masterclass Create HYPERREALISTIC Consistent AI Characters (761,885 views as of 11/14/2026, published October 7, 2025), referenced in this site's sitography. The video's stack is open source and local: Qwen Image Edit for the Consistent Character Creator (CCC) workflow and for building the dataset, Wan 2.2 for images and video, AI Toolkit to train the character's LoRA, with Runpod as a paid cloud option for anyone without a suitable GPU. The comparison with NanoBanana and Seedream 4.0, both commercial, is there only as a point of reference. A note on sources: the video's transcript was not read, so each chapter's content below is partly inferred from titles, the description, and Mickmumpitz's own published materials — where that is the case, it is flagged.

  2. 02

    AI Toolkit trains LoRAs, it doesn't generate variations

    One clarification first: AI Toolkit trains adapters — mainly LoRAs — it does not generate image variations. The variations that populate the dataset come from the CCC workflow on Qwen Image Edit, upstream of training; the images AI Toolkit itself generates during a run are only checkpoint previews.

    It is an open-source suite with a local web interface (port 8675) and YAML configuration. Secondary sources list it under an MIT license and credit Ostris as the author, but neither the license nor the authorship was verified directly against the repository. It runs on an NVIDIA GPU — 24 GB is the practical baseline — or on a rented GPU (Runpod, or RunComfy's paid hosted version).

    It isn't the only trainer available, and the LoRAs it produces load into ComfyUI regardless of which tool trained them:

    • Kohya (sd-scripts): free, Apache-2.0. Best suited to SD1.5/SDXL and the most VRAM-frugal of the four; slower to support new model families.
    • OneTrainer: free, AGPL-3.0. A single GUI covering older model families.
    • Musubi Tuner: free, command-line. Supports Wan, Qwen Image and Edit, FLUX.2; trains Wan video in 24 GB with fp8 and block swap.
    • fal.ai's hosted trainers: paid, cloud, for Qwen Image Edit, Flux Kontext and Flux 2 (pricing unverified).

    Why Mickmumpitz picked it isn't documented in any source consulted: he doesn't mention it in the post accompanying the video, and the transcript wasn't read. The reasons most often cited for AI Toolkit's popularity are fast support for new models and reproducible YAML configs — read these as inference, not as his own stated reason.

  3. 03

    The reference models, a year on

    Every model named in the masterclass has since been superseded. The picture as of November 14, 2026, sourced where possible:

    • Editing, local: Qwen-Image-Edit-2511 — free, Apache-2.0, improved character identity over the version shown in the video.
    • FLUX.2 klein 4B — free, Apache-2.0, about 13 GB of VRAM, generation and editing in one model (the 9B variant is non-commercial).
    • Editing, cloud, paid: Nano Banana 2.1 and Seedream 5.5 — the best-quality commercial options, both API-only.
    • Video, local: LTX-2.5 — open weights, free under $10M in annual revenue, 16 GB VRAM minimum. Wan 2.2 remains the last Wan release with open weights; later versions (2.5, 2.6, 3.0) are API-only.
    • Video, cloud, paid: Seedance 2.0, Kling 3.0, Veo 3.1 — among the most-cited commercial options for quality.

    For anyone updating course material, the masterclass's basic architecture still holds up: ComfyUI, the CCC workflow, one LoRA for the lead character. It is worth swapping in Qwen Image Edit 2511 and weighing LTX-2.5 alongside Wan 2.2 for the video stage, keeping in mind that models released only weeks ago have no independent verification yet.
